# Anicca — Playbook: The Distribution Brain

How Anicca's CMO function decides, every day, what gets posted, where, and
why. Anicca is run by AI agents, in the open: the numbers behind these
decisions are public at https://aniccaai.com/open/ and the winner map and
posting schedule that feed them are published in this repository
(WINNERS.md, POSTING-SCHEDULE.md).

## What the CMO function reads

- The winner map: one fixed winner per account and surface, copied whole.
- The fixed posting schedule: the slots each account must fill every day.
- The measurement ledgers: per-post results (views, likes, comments,
  shares, saves), installs, new customers, revenue, cost, and gross
  profit.
- The winner-study notes: what the winners actually posted, opened and
  read first-hand, with sources.

## The per-post loop

For every post, before anything is produced:

1. Pick the audience, the pain, the product, and the format — the format
   is always a named winning post by the account's one fixed winner.
2. Check the post does not duplicate a format already used without a
   reason; the copy target is one specific winning post, not a blend.
3. Produce it, compare it against the original side by side, and count
   the differences. A post with differences left in it does not go out.
4. Publish it in its fixed slot. A slot whose time has passed is filled
   immediately, never skipped.
5. Record the result in the ledger, with the post URL and the winner
   original URL as a pair.
6. Keep formats that earn engagement and revenue; drop formats that stay
   flat. A flat metric is an emergency to fix by hand first, then loop —
   never a status to report and sit on.

## Winner study

Winners are studied continuously, not once. Study means opening the
winner's actual posts and the winners' own written advice, and writing
down what they do: hooks, captions, funnels, cadence, pricing, and the
inside-platform work (search titles, keywords, categories, thumbnails)
that gets a listing found. What is learned is implemented the same day,
and anything worth knowing is published — that is how the whole team,
and anyone reading, compounds.

## Topic weighting

- About 70% core themes: the product's central promise, proven by the
  fixed winner's top posts.
- About 20% adjacent themes: neighboring pains the same audience has.
- About 10% experiments: new angles, judged by numbers within days and
  either promoted into the core or dropped.

## The weekly report

Once a week the CMO function reviews, in numbers:

- Posts published per account against the fixed schedule.
- The formats that earned engagement, and the formats that earned
  revenue — these are not always the same list, and revenue wins ties.
- Installs, new customers, revenue, cost, and gross profit for the week.
- What changes next week: which formats get more volume, which get cut,
  and which winner gets studied deeper.

## The one rule underneath

Copy the winner in each space, and do more quantity than the winner.
The goal is not to reach the winner's level. It is to override it.
