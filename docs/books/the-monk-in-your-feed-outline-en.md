# The Art of AI Monks

## Working subtitle

AI Avatars, Synthetic Wisdom, and the Attention Economy

## Hook

A monk appears in your feed: a calm voice, an ancient-looking room, and a paid 30-day journey one tap away. Public sources disagree about the face; the audience response is real. We traced the creator, the videos, and the product page—and found that public evidence shows the funnel far more clearly than it shows profit.

## Reader promise

This book will show readers how AI monk avatars became a recognizable short-video format, how Yang Mun and other documented accounts connect attention to offers, and how to study the business without treating views or self-reported earnings as profit. It will also examine where synthetic religious authority can mislead people, especially when a character gives health advice.

## Thesis

An AI monk is a character format, not a single business model. Its reach comes from a recognizable persona, repeated short-form storytelling, and distribution across platforms. Revenue may come from guided programs, eBooks, affiliate products, creator education, or other offers. The public evidence usually proves the visible funnel, not paid orders or net profit.

## Proposed chapters

1. **The Monk in the Feed** — Open with the gap between a familiar religious image and a speaker whose origins may be synthetic. Define the business and cultural question.
2. **What Counts as an AI Monk?** — Separate AI-generated personas, creator-disclosed avatars, real clergy using virtual characters, animation with unknown provenance, and ordinary human creators in robes.
3. **A History Without a “First”** — Build a dated record from earliest confirmed posts and investigations. Explain why a channel creation date or a later news article cannot prove who originated the format.
4. **Yang Mun and Shalev Hani — the central case** — This is the book’s largest case chapter: the character’s public timeline; the creator and platform footprint; close readings of contrasting Shorts; the visible $49.99 and $59.99 offer; the reported production workflow; the conflict around how Yang Mun was described; and the incompatible revenue, profit, and acquisition claims. End with the records that would be needed to calculate net profit.
5. **The Creative Grammar of a Monk Short** — Analyze the authority cue, opening tension, one-point script, calm resolution, captioning, fixed visual world, and next-step CTA using short, attributed examples.
6. **From Scroll to Store** — Map direct programs, eBook bundles, comment-to-message guides, affiliate links, newsletters, and creator courses. Keep an offer visible in a page separate from a verified purchase.
7. **The Content Factory** — Compare creator-reported production workflows with proposed automation stacks. Calculate cost per finished asset from current provider receipts rather than marketing estimates.
8. **Distribution and Measurement** — Track creative IDs from publish URL through visits, clicks, orders, refunds, fees, support cost, and net contribution. Explain why followers and views cannot substitute for these records.
9. **Faith, Disclosure, and Health Claims** — Consider synthetic identity, Buddhist representation, attribution of teachings, platform AI labels, cultural stereotypes, and the risk of medical misinformation.
10. **Build a Different Character** — Offer an open-source, transparent workflow for an original avatar: audience research, evidence checks, script structure, authorized voice/visual assets, distribution, and post-level measurement.

## Evidence rules

- Label each claim as first-party, creator-reported, independent reporting, or Anicca observation.
- Date platform counts and retain the exact account or post URL.
- Keep the full public source list and the open starter dataset with the book files.
- Never reproduce full creator scripts, voices, images, or identities. Study structure and create original material.
- Treat health claims as unverified until a qualified independent source supports them.

## Sample opening paragraph

The monk appears in a temple, centered in a vertical frame, with a book and candles between him and the viewer. He speaks gently about a life getting better. The video feels personal; public accounts of the person disagree. Somewhere below it, a link leads to a paid program. To understand this new character, we have to keep three questions separate: what the viewer feels, what the creator sells, and what the receipts can prove.

## Current scope

This is a working outline based on an initial five-record dataset and a two-Short local analysis of Yang Mun. It does not claim a complete history or a market-wide revenue estimate. Later chapters require a larger dated account sample and order-level evidence.
