# Anicca — Playbook: What $10K MRR Founders Actually Did

This is the evidence base behind our app strategy: cases collected
from founders' own public posts about reaching roughly $10K in
monthly recurring revenue, read in full, with the pattern extracted
and the action written down. It is the standing answer to "why do
we work this way?" — because this is what measurably worked.

## How cases are graded

- **A** — the founder's own post, with numbers in it.
- **B** — the founder's own post, thin on detail.
- **C** — secondhand or estimated. Always labelled, never quoted
  as fact.

Only what a post actually states is recorded. Anything not stated
is "unknown", however plausible it sounds.

## The patterns, ranked by how many cases show them

1. **Copy a proven format and post it daily.** The growth cases
   repeat a competitor's proven post format, two to five posts a
   day across accounts, for months. None of them credit a single
   viral hit; the compounding of an ordinary, copied format is the
   whole story.
2. **ASO, ratings, and localisation compound slowly, then fast.**
   The clearest case took about two and a half years to reach
   $10K MRR on search traffic, ratings, and localisation — and
   about two more months to double. Search is slow money that
   becomes big money.
3. **Fix monetisation before chasing more traffic.** Switching
   from a free plan to a free trial was reported to raise MRR by
   about 207% in one case. Weekly plans and a hard paywall recur
   across the subscription cases. The failure case is equally
   clear: roughly 1% download-to-paid conversion does not grow,
   however much traffic arrives.
4. **Show the outcome.** Posts that demonstrate the result the
   product produces outperform posts that describe features, and
   every serious case judges content by conversion, not views.
5. **No paid growth.** Under a zero-spend rule, paid influencers
   and ads appear in these cases only as things the winners
   skipped.

## What this means for an app

- Run the copy loop daily: one fixed winner, their winning posts,
  our version out the same day, measured per post.
- Run several accounts and a second language as separate surfaces,
  each with its own winner — never one creative sprayed across all.
- Keep a daily content unit tied to the subscription, and an
  ASO + ratings + localisation pack worked every week.
- Keep a conversion scoreboard: installs and new customers per
  post, per template, per channel. Kill by conversion.

## What this means for an agent product

- Publish proof-of-work posts with real numbers, in the open.
- Let new users try the core capability without a card first.
- Teach in public: tutorials and search pages that answer the
  exact questions buyers type.
- Report the numbers weekly, in public, whether they flatter us
  or not. The scoreboard is the marketing.

## The one rule underneath

Nothing here is a theory. Each pattern is in the file because a
founder published it with numbers attached, and each pattern stays
only while our own measurements agree with it.
